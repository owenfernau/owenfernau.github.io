title: Information Delivery Systems
---

Information is even less of a bottleneck than it was pre-AI (but post internet). LLMs can produce more well-informed, efficient-to-understand responses than 99% of people already (in mid-2026). Therefore, given that more people may get "better" "pure information" by talking to an AI, we should potentially view "writing" as somewhat less important for "pure information" transfer (though learning by writing, reading novels, still matter as much as ever).

Potentially, this means we should turn our focus more to "information routing" rather than information itself. It's more important (unlocks more economic growth/social value) that the "right" information finds the "right" people (information that "changes lives") rather than someone writes a blog post that doesn't uncover anything novel or useful. It's about the right post finding the right person such that that person applies to work for a professor whose they would've otherwise been unaware of. 

In a way, this is the same as it always was: writing is often about about "finding the others," not about being useful information. Though I think, when the printing press was invented, that probably really was about archiving information such that we didn't lose track of it. Now, given that AIs will produce more written words than people, we're "somewhere else." 

This isn't to say factual works aren't useful. The informational curation an individual does on the way to writing a non-fiction work, for example, is important and worth exploring. I just think we're at a point where, projects/technology, should focus on "content routing." Because people are writing (including myself) partially to find people to work with, to prove competence, rather than to become famous or something.
