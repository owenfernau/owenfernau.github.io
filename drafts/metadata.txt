The strange thing about posting online is that the "metadata" around what a person says works differently than it does in person. Face to face, metadata essentially is the context around someone saying something. A simple "hi" means different things based on factors like tone of voice, prior relationship with the speaker, and a myriad of other things that people often sum up with the words "vibe" or "aura."

In person, it's impossible to have two identical "hi's" from different people (or even the same person on different days), because the context is inextricable from what's being said. The written (or typed) word, on the other hand, lets the information (for example, "hi") be separated from its context (the metadata).

Written communication has its own metadata. Some of it is aesthetic: the text's font, formatting, how the writing looks before you read a word. There's also the context of where text is placed: who says it, when they say it, and where in the media landscape they say it. 

The digital world introduces an interesting phenomenon: one "hi" can be identical to another, down to the 0s and 1s.
 
What differs is the metadata attached. On social media, posts have likes and comments. Posts are attached to profiles, which have their own context of followers, prior content, a text-based bio, and more. That metadata influences how a platform's users receive information. A famous actor tweeting "good morning" the day after a DUI means something different than a politician tweeting "good morning" after winning an election.

So people looking to influence the attention they receive online would do well to optimize their metadata as well as what they post. Each platform has its own metadata, with some crossover. Tech Twitter and LinkedIn care about universities. Tech Twitter especially also cares about which companies you've worked at. I'm sure Instagram and TikTok have their own metadata language, but I'm not very familiar with those platforms.

I'm writing this essay in the tradition of writing to your younger self: if you're going to use social media, understand explicitly the framework of metadata which a given platform rewards in addition to the content you post. In a world where it's clear not all "hi's" are created equal, try to understand what differentiates them and decide whether you want to play that game.
